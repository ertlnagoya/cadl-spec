Appendix J — Grounding of the Institutional Parameters in Norms (grounding 0.1)

This appendix specifies the grounding extension to CADL. It builds on the norms extension (Appendix F) and adds the key grounding: to contract_def (A.4), the keys shares: and on_fulfilment: to norm_def and the key magnitude: to on_violation: (F.3), and, in a file that also uses the strategic model extension (Appendix I), the keys sanction_of: and reward_of: to a payoff term (I.2). With them, a processor computes from the norms of a contract two derived indices, α̂ and β̂, and the incentive coverage χ, all in [0, 1], and compares the declared alpha and beta with the indices. The extension also defines the weight of a payoff term that is declared to stand for the sanction of a norm in a strategic model: the size the norm declares, multiplied by λ.

The core language does not mandate these keys; a conforming CADL processor that does not implement the extension MUST NOT reject a file because of them. It SHOULD accept them syntactically and emit an informational diagnostic.

The extension is named grounding and this appendix describes version 0.1. It requires norms: 0.1. A file that uses it MUST declare both under extensions: (A.2); J.12 says what a processor does with an undeclared use. The keys sanction_of: and reward_of: additionally require stochastic: 0.1 and games: 0.1.

sos:
  ...
  extensions:
    - norms: 0.1
    - grounding: 0.1

J.1 Motivation and scope​

Section 5.4.2 defines the institutional parameters α, β, and λ as numbers in [0, 1] with qualitative end points, and nothing else in the language says what an intermediate value means. A designer writes beta: 0.7, and no rule relates that number to the contract in which it stands. Appendix F states an intended reading (β describes who holds the powers of a contract, α which triggers and contents the other parties can observe, and λ scales the sanctions of violated norms) and leaves it informative; Section 9.6 lists the formal link between norms and the parameters as open, and RD-1.5 asks for λ to be treated as a parameter of the contracts. The classification of institutional rules by what they affect, among them information rules, aggregation rules, and payoff rules [Ostrom, 2005], suggests that the three parameters summarise three kinds of norms. Grounding 0.1 is a first step. It does not give α, β, and λ a formal meaning in terms of norms; that remains open beyond what follows.

  • Two indices and a coverage are computed from the file. J.4 defines α̂, β̂, and χ as ratios of counts over finite sets that are read from one contract. They are reproducible in the sense that two processors compute the same numbers from the same file. For α̂ both counts rest on annotations of the author (the items and the shares: lists), so the index is as good as the annotation.
  • Indices are evidence, not meaning. The indices do not replace the declared parameters and do not change Section 5.4.2. They are structural facts about the norms of a contract with which a declared value can agree or disagree. J.4 says what they cannot capture.
  • Declared values are compared with them. A disagreement between a declared α or β and its index is a warning that names the construct concerned (J.5). No rule of this appendix makes a file invalid that is valid under Appendix F and uses none of the new keys (J.12).
  • λ is a scale. In this appendix λ multiplies the sizes of sanctions and rewards; it is not compared with a derived index. χ measures something else: how many norms carry a sanction or a reward that λ could scale.
  • Sanctions get a size, and the model uses it. magnitude: gives the sanction or the reward of a norm a number. In a strategic model, a payoff term declared with sanction_of: has, by definition, the weight −magnitude·λ, where λ is the parameter bound to the lambda of the contract (J.7). That the term represents the sanction, that is, that its reward structure counts the violations of the norm, remains the author's assumption, and every result computed with such a term is reported with it.

Scope of version 0.1.

  • An index concerns one contract, and the same three quantities are also defined over all contracts of a file (J.4, "The quantities of the SoS"). No declared value is compared with these, and no weighted index is defined.
  • Information items are declared by the author (grounding.items); they are not derived from actor interfaces, predicates, or messages.
  • The indices are counts. They are not weighted by magnitudes, by severities, or by the number of norms a power affects.
  • magnitude: and on_fulfilment: have no effect on the dynamic semantics of Appendix F: verdicts, violations, and reparations are unchanged.
  • The extension does not derive a model from norms and does not check that a reward structure of a model counts the violations of a norm (H.8 defines no translation between a norm and a model).
  • No index is defined for the delegations of Appendix G or for the motivation parameters ρ and κ of Appendix C.

J.2 Concepts​

ConceptMeaning in grounding 0.1
Actor nameThe identifier of an actor reference, without its index: ROBOT for ROBOT[i], "ROBOT[*]", and ROBOT[2].
Party nameThe actor name of an entry of parties: of the contract. Entries with one actor name are one party.
Single actorAn actor reference with an integer index, or without an index when the actor is declared without a range.
Information itemA named piece of information declared in grounding.items, with its origin, the party from which it stems. An item has one origin; information that stems from two parties is declared as two items.
Sharing normAn obligation or a permission whose content is a message and which has shares:; its message gives the listed items to the receiver.
MagnitudeA number that is not negative: the size of a sanction or of a reward at full incentive intensity (λ = 1), in the unit of account of the contract.
Derived indexα̂ or β̂: a rational number in [0, 1] computed from one contract, or undefined.
Incentive coverageχ: the fraction of the obligations and prohibitions of a contract that carry a sanction or a reward, or undefined.

Norm, obligation, permission, prohibition, power, and bearer have the meaning of Appendix F.

J.3 Syntax (EBNF additions to A.4, F.3, and I.2)​

The grammar uses the notation of Appendix A, which defines identifier, actor_ref, number, and text.

contract_def    = ... , [ "grounding:" , grounding_block ] ;
grounding_block = [ "items:"     , { identifier , ":" , actor_ref } ] ,
                  [ "tolerance:" , number ] ;

norm_def        = ... ,
                  [ "shares:"        , { "-" , identifier } ] ,
                  [ "on_fulfilment:" , norm_fulfilment ] ;
norm_violation  = ... , [ "magnitude:" , number ] ;
norm_fulfilment = [ "reward:"    , text ] ,
                  [ "magnitude:" , number ] ;

payoff_term     = "reward:" , identifier ,
                  ( [ "weight:" , param_expr ]                  (* I.2 *)
                  | "sanction_of:" , norm_ref
                  | "reward_of:"   , norm_ref ) ;
norm_ref        = identifier , "." , identifier ;               (* string *)
  • grounding.items maps the name of each information item to its origin. tolerance: is a number in [0, 1], by default 0.25 (J.5).
  • shares: lists the information items that the message of a sharing norm conveys.
  • on_violation.magnitude is the size of the sanction of the norm.
  • on_fulfilment: records a reward for fulfilling the norm: reward: describes it in text, as sanction: describes a sanction, and magnitude: is its size. The reward is deemed paid when the norm instance reaches the state fulfilled of F.5: for an obligation, when its content is brought about in time; for a prohibition, when its window closes or the contract instance ends without a violation. It is not paid when the instance is discharged, although the verdict of a discharged instance is fulfilled. The block is an annotation and has no effect on the semantics of Appendix F.
  • A norm_ref is a quoted string "C.n": the id of a contract, a dot, and the id of a norm of that contract.

A text that is empty or consists of white space only (sanction: "", reward: " ") counts as empty, and a YAML null counts as absent, for every key of this appendix. A text is taken after YAML loading: an unquoted scalar that the loader reads as a number or a boolean is the text as written (sanction: 0 is the text "0" and carries an incentive), and an author SHOULD quote such a text. A reward: that is a sequence or a mapping is an error (J-4). A magnitude MUST be written as a decimal: a YAML 1.1 loader reads 1e3 as a string, which is an error of J-4, and 1.0e-7 as a number. An unquoted item name that a YAML 1.1 loader reads as a boolean or null MUST be quoted (A.12).

J.4 The derived indices and the coverage​

The three quantities are defined for every contract C of a file that declares the extension. Each is a ratio of two counts, or undefined when its denominator is zero. An undefined quantity takes part in no comparison of J.5.

Information sharing: α̂​

Let Π be the set of party names of C, X the set of items of grounding.items, and o(x) the origin of an item x (a party name, J-2). The pairs of C are the pairs (x, q) of an item x and a party name q other than o(x): the places where an item can be shared with a party.

Whether a target stands for a whole party follows the matching of actor references of F.3. An actor reference T denotes the whole party q when its actor name is q and it is a single actor A (J.2), A[*], A[i] with an identifier as index, the full range with which the actor is declared, or a range with a symbolic bound (which matches every member, F.3). A reference with an integer index denotes the whole party only when it is equal to the only entry of parties: with that actor name. Any other reference with an integer index or a range is member-specific. A bare set name, A for an actor declared with a range, matches no member (F.3): it gives no pair, and a processor SHOULD warn (J-3); it is not member-specific. Indices and ranges are compared as written; the core parser does not accept white space inside an index. For example, with parties: [DISPATCHER, "ROBOT[1]", "ROBOT[2]"] the target ROBOT[2] is member-specific, and only ROBOT[*], ROBOT[i], or a range gives the pair of an item and ROBOT.

A pair (x, q) is given

  • by a sharing entry, when an entry "S -> T : x" of information.sharing has the item x and a target T that denotes the whole party q; an entry that is not of this form with one item, such as "A -> B : x, y" or "A -> B : f(5s)", which the core parser accepts leniently (A.12), gives no pair, and a processor SHOULD warn (J-3);
  • by an obligation, or by a permission, when a sharing norm of that kind lists x in shares: and has as content a message S -> T : m whose receiver T denotes the whole party q.

A pair is given when it is given in at least one of the three ways.

α̂  =  (number of given pairs) / (number of pairs)

α̂ is undefined when there is no pair (no item, or a single party name). α̂ = 0 exactly when no pair is given, which corresponds, for the declared items only, to the end point "no sharing" of 5.4.2; α̂ = 1 exactly when every pair is given, which corresponds, for the declared items only, to "complete sharing". A processor reports, with α̂, the number of pairs given in each of the three ways, the pairs that are not given, and the member-specific entries and norms: an entry or norm that would give a pair but whose target is member-specific (as in "DISPATCHER -> ROBOT[3] : route") gives no pair and is listed separately, each entry and each norm once, however many items it names. A contract that declares alpha: 0 and whose sharing entries are all member-specific has α̂ = 0 and gets no warning of J-6, although a member-specific entry is sharing; the index does not see it.

What the definition does not look at:

  • The source S is not examined: an entry gives the item to T whoever sends it.
  • An identifier index always counts as the whole party, also where the author means one selected member (ROBOT[winner]).
  • A target that is not a party of the contract gives no pair; a processor SHOULD warn (J-3).
  • The state of a norm is ignored: a sharing norm counts whether or not it has a trigger:, an until:, or a deadline:, and whether it is active from the start or only as a reparation or by a power.
  • A prohibition of the same message does not remove a pair; N-12 of Appendix F warns about such a conflict.
  • An obligation and a permission both give a pair. The index records that a norm makes or lets a party have an item, not that the party has it; the three counts keep the difference visible.
  • A contract whose parties are one actor set has a single party name and no pair, so α̂ is undefined for it. Sharing among peers, which is what matters in a collaborative SoS of equal constituents, is therefore not measured in version 0.1.

Concentration of powers: β̂​

The decision points of C are its powers (F.2). A power is the only construct of a contract whose exercise changes the state of other norms with a defined effect (F.5); authority.decision_scope is text and is not counted. For a single actor a (J.2), let k(a) be the number of powers of C whose bearer: is the reference a (the same actor name and the same index). A power whose bearer is not a single actor, as ROBOT[i], is held by each member for itself and counts for no single actor.

β̂  =  (largest k(a) over the single actors a) / (number of powers)

β̂ is 0 when no power is borne by a single actor, and undefined only when C has no power. It is the largest share of the decision points of the contract that lie with one actor: a concentration index. β̂ = 1 exactly when one single actor bears every power, which corresponds, for the powers only, to the end point "single actor decides all" of 5.4.2; β̂ = 0 exactly when no single actor bears a power.

β̂ does not depend on authority.decision_holder. A processor reports with β̂: the number of powers per bearer; for each power, the number of distinct norms named in its effects:; and, when the decision holder names a single actor h, the share of the decision holder k(h) divided by the number of powers. The counts are not weighted by the effects: a power that discharges one norm and a power that discharges another party's power count alike.

Incentive coverage: χ​

Let N be the set of obligations and prohibitions of C. A norm of N carries an incentive when at least one of the following holds:

  • it has an on_violation.sanction that is not empty;
  • it has an on_violation.magnitude greater than zero;
  • it has an on_fulfilment.reward that is not empty, or an on_fulfilment.magnitude greater than zero.
χ  =  (number of norms of N that carry an incentive) / (number of norms in N)

χ is undefined when N is empty. χ = 0 exactly when no obligation or prohibition carries a sanction or a reward, and χ = 1 exactly when each of them does. χ is not an index of λ. It counts the norms that have something for λ to scale; λ says by how much (J.7).

Two further counts are reported separately and are not part of χ: the norms of N that carry no incentive and have an on_violation.reparation (their violation creates a further duty, not a sanction), and the norms of N that have an on_violation.transition (their violation moves or ends the contract instance). severity: is not counted anywhere: it classifies a violation.

How the quantities move​

The three quantities are invariant under renaming and under reordering of norms, items, and parties. They are not invariant under splitting or merging: one power written as two, or two obligations merged into one, changes β̂ or χ although the contract asks the same of the parties. In each lemma everything not mentioned stays the same, and the quantity is defined before and after.

  • Lemma 1 (α̂). Adding an entry to information.sharing, adding an item to the shares: of a sharing norm, or adding a sharing norm never lowers α̂. Proof. The set of pairs is unchanged, and every pair that was given is still given. Adding an item or a party, in contrast, adds pairs and can lower α̂.
  • Lemma 2 (β̂). Adding a power whose bearer is a single actor with the largest k(a) never lowers β̂, and adding a power whose bearer is not a single actor never raises it. Proof. With the largest count k among n powers, k ≤ n, so k/n ≤ (k + 1)/(n + 1) and k/(n + 1) ≤ k/n.
  • Lemma 3 (χ). Making an obligation or a prohibition carry an incentive that carried none (by a sanction text that is not empty, or a magnitude greater than zero) raises χ by 1/|N|; a magnitude of zero does not. Adding a norm to N that carries an incentive never lowers χ, and adding one that carries none never raises it. Proof. As for Lemma 2.

What the quantities do not capture​

  • α̂ counts entitlements and duties to share, not what a party observes in operation, and not the quality or the delay of the information. The items are those the author lists.
  • β̂ counts powers. Decisions that are not institutional acts, such as who plans a route, are not norms and are not counted; all powers weigh the same, whatever their effects.
  • χ says how many norms carry a sanction or a reward, not how large these are or how likely they are to be applied.
  • Only the product of a magnitude and λ enters any result of J.7. Within one contract, λ and the magnitudes are not identified separately: doubling every magnitude and halving λ changes nothing.
  • The core keys incentives.type, incentives.rules, and violation.action are text and are ignored; a contract can have incentives there that χ does not see.
  • Under the enforcement of Appendix G, a prohibited message is denied at the boundary and cannot be sent, so the prohibition needs no sanction to be effective. A coverage of 0 in an enforced contract is therefore not "no incentive".
  • No quantity says anything about behaviour. Whether more sharing or a stronger incentive improves a property is a question for the models of Appendices H and I.

The quantities of the SoS​

The three quantities are also defined over all contracts C₁, …, Cₘ of a file with m ≥ 2, that is, over one version of the SoS; an amended version (F.6) has its own. The definitions take the counts of the contracts together.

  • α̂_SoS. An item is qualified by its contract. The pairs of the SoS are the triples (Cᵢ, x, q) of a contract and a pair (x, q) of Cᵢ, and such a triple is given when (x, q) is given in Cᵢ. An item named in two contracts gives pairs in each, whether its origins are the same or not, and an entry or a sharing norm of one contract gives no pair of another: the parties of the other contract have not agreed to it, and v0.3 has no identity of items across contracts.

    α̂_SoS  =  Σᵢ (given pairs of Cᵢ) / Σᵢ (pairs of Cᵢ)

  • β̂_SoS. The decision points of the SoS are the powers of all contracts. For a single actor a, K(a) = Σᵢ kᵢ(a) is the number of powers that a bears in all contracts, with kᵢ the count k of J.4 in Cᵢ (the same actor name and the same index).

    β̂_SoS  =  (largest K(a) over the single actors a) / Σᵢ (powers of Cᵢ)

    It is the largest share of all powers of the SoS that one single actor bears.

  • χ_SoS. N_SoS is the union of the sets N of the contracts.

    χ_SoS  =  Σᵢ (norms of Nᵢ that carry an incentive) / Σᵢ |Nᵢ|

Each is undefined when its denominator is zero. A processor reports them with the sums of the counts of J-5 that apply (the three ways of giving a pair, the member-specific entries and norms, the powers per bearer, the norms with a reparation only and with a transition); there is no decision holder of the SoS. When several single actors have the largest K, all of them are named, sorted by name. The quantities and their report are invariant under reordering the contracts, and those of each contract are unchanged.

No weights. A weighted index, which would count a contract or a norm more than another, is not defined, because every weight would be an arbitrary constant that the file does not contain.

No comparison. v0.3 declares α, β, and λ per contract, and the SoS has no declared value of them. No rule of J.5 or J.8 compares α̂_SoS, β̂_SoS, or χ_SoS with anything; they are reported only.

How these quantities relate to those of the contracts, with everything not mentioned unchanged:

  • Lemma 4 (bounds). When defined, α̂_SoS lies between the least and the largest α̂ of the contracts where α̂ is defined, and χ_SoS likewise. β̂_SoS is at most Σᵢ (largest kᵢ) / Σᵢ (powers of Cᵢ), the mean of the β̂ of the contracts weighted by their numbers of powers, and so at most the largest β̂; it equals that mean exactly when one single actor has the largest count in every contract or no single actor bears a power. Proof. A ratio of sums lies between the ratios. K(a) = Σᵢ kᵢ(a) ≤ Σᵢ (largest kᵢ), with equality exactly when kᵢ(a) is the largest in every Cᵢ.
  • β̂_SoS can be smaller than every β̂ of a contract, and is not a function of them. Counterexample. Two contracts, in each of which one single actor bears the only power: each has β̂ = 1. When the actor is X in both, β̂_SoS = 2/2 = 1; when it is X in one and Y in the other, β̂_SoS = 1/2.
  • Lemmas 1 and 3 carry over. Adding an entry, an item of shares:, or a sharing norm to any contract never lowers α̂_SoS. Making an obligation or a prohibition of any contract carry an incentive that carried none raises χ_SoS by 1/|N_SoS|. The proofs are those of J.4: the set of pairs, and N_SoS, are unchanged.
  • Lemma 2 carries over with K in place of k. Adding a power whose bearer is a single actor with the largest K never lowers β̂_SoS, and adding a power whose bearer is not a single actor never raises it. The condition of Lemma 2 within the contract is not enough. Counterexample. C₁ has two powers of X and C₂ one power of Y, so β̂_SoS = 2/3. A second power of Y in C₂, where Y has the largest count, leaves the β̂ of C₂ at 1 and lowers β̂_SoS to 2/4.
  • Adding a contract. When a contract C′ is added and α̂_SoS and the α̂ of C′ are defined, the new α̂_SoS lies between the old one and the α̂ of C′, and likewise for χ (a ratio of sums). Adding a contract can therefore lower α̂_SoS and χ_SoS. Counterexample. A contract with one item that it shares with the other party has α̂ = 1; adding a contract that declares one item and shares nothing gives α̂_SoS = 1/2. Adding a contract can also lower β̂_SoS, as in the counterexample after Lemma 4. It never raises β̂_SoS above the larger of its old value and the β̂ of C′, by the mediant inequality: for every single actor a, (K(a) + k′(a)) / (n + n′) lies between K(a)/n, which is at most the old β̂_SoS, and k′(a)/n′, which is at most the β̂ of C′, where n and n′ are the numbers of powers.

J.5 Relation to the declared parameters​

A processor computes the three quantities for every contract and reports them (J-5). For alpha and beta it reports the declared value, the index, and their difference, and compares them. Every disagreement is a warning: the indices are evidence about the norms, and declaring this extension never invalidates a file that declares norms: 0.1.

A declared end point with a counterexample in the contract. The end points of 5.4.2 are universal statements. When a construct of the same contract is a counterexample, a processor SHOULD warn and name the construct (J-6):

DeclaredCounterexample named in the warning
alpha: 0a given pair, with the entry or sharing norm that gives it; when α̂ is undefined, a non-empty information.sharing
alpha: 1the pairs that are not given (absence of a norm does not show that a party lacks an item, so this is the weakest of the four)
beta: 1a power that is not borne by a single actor with the largest count (the first in the order of the file when several have it); every power when β̂ = 0
beta: 0a single actor with the largest count, but only when β̂ exceeds 1 divided by the number of distinct single actors that bear a power

β̂ is 0 only when every power is borne by an actor set. A contract in which several single actors each bear the same number of powers and none bears more has β̂ equal to 1 divided by their number, the least value possible for these bearers, and beta: 0 gets no warning there.

An intermediate value far from the index. For α and β, a processor SHOULD warn when the declared value and the index differ by more than the tolerance of the contract and J-6 gives no warning for that parameter (J-7). The default tolerance is 0.25: the indices are coarse, and a contract with few norms has few possible index values (with two powers, β̂ is 0, 1/2, or 1). An author who accepts a larger difference states it with grounding.tolerance. A processor SHOULD also warn when the decision holder names a single actor whose share is smaller than β̂: the powers are then concentrated in another actor.

λ is not compared with a number. λ is a scale, and χ is a coverage; a contract in which every norm is sanctioned mildly has χ = 1 and a small λ and is consistent. A processor reports χ next to the declared lambda and SHOULD warn in two cases only (J-8): lambda is greater than zero while no obligation or prohibition of the contract carries an incentive (χ = 0, or N is empty), so that λ has nothing to scale in the norms; and lambda: 0 while some norm carries an incentive, so that what the norm declares has no effect in a model.

Versions of a contract. A processor that compares two versions of a contract (F.6) reports what changed in the keys of this appendix (magnitudes, on_fulfilment:, shares:, items) and SHOULD warn when a declared value and its index move in opposite directions (J-13): for example, beta is raised while an amendment gives a power to a robot.

J.6 The parameters in the models of Appendices H and I​

Today. A parameter of a model is bound to a declared value by from: "C.alpha", "C.beta", or "C.lambda" (H.3), so that contract and model use the same number. What the number does in the model is written by the author: in H.12, β is the probability that a decision is taken centrally and α multiplies the quality of the central plan. Neither appendix derives these expressions from the contract, and this appendix does not either.

What this appendix adds for α and β. The norms of a contract describe the design in which the bound parameter has its declared value. A regime that assigns another value describes an operating mode (H.6) for which the file has no norms of its own. A processor therefore compares the fixed value of a parameter bound to C.alpha or C.beta with the index of C in every design, and SHOULD emit an informational diagnostic, not a warning, for a design in which they differ by more than the tolerance (J-9).

What stays open. A regime has no norms of its own, so the values that regimes assign to α and β cannot be checked against indices, and the ordering of regimes by β cannot be compared with an ordering of their norms. Deriving a probability of the model from a sharing norm or from a power, and α as what a player observes in a game (RD-7.3), are not defined.

J.7 λ: from the sanction of a norm to a payoff term​

In a strategic model (Appendix I), a payoff term may name the norm whose sanction or reward it is declared to represent, instead of giving a weight:

- { reward: shortcuts, sanction_of: "DELIVERY.no_shortcut" }

Let n be the norm C.n that the term names, and let ℓ be the parameter of the model that is bound by from: "C.lambda" (there MUST be exactly one, J-10). The term has no weight:; its weight is defined as

TermWeight
sanction_of: "C.n"−m · ℓ, with m the on_violation.magnitude of n
reward_of: "C.n"m′ · ℓ, with m′ the on_fulfilment.magnitude of n

The term is otherwise a term of I.6, and Appendix I applies to it unchanged.

The assumption that is not checked. The reward structure of the term is the author's statement of how often the sanction is applied, or the reward paid, in the model. The convention is: the accumulated reward of a sanction_of: term counts the violations of the norm, and that of a reward_of: term its fulfilments, over all contract instances within one run of the model; a run may span several contract instances. No processor can check this. Appendix F judges a norm on a timed trace and Appendix H evaluates a model in steps, and no translation between the two is defined. Every result computed with a payoff that has such a term MUST therefore be reported together with the assumption, for each term: "the reward structure r is taken to count the violations (fulfilments) of C.n" (J-12).

Who bears it. A sanction is a loss of the bearer of the norm: a sanction_of: term stands in the payoff of the player that stands for the bearer, and nowhere else. This appendix does not say where the amount goes. When it is a transfer to the counterparty, the author writes a second, ordinary term in the payoff of the counterparty's player, with the same reward structure and an explicit weight (weight: "2 * lambda"); the size of that term is not checked against the norm (J-11).

What a processor checks (J-10, J-11). The norm exists, is an obligation or a prohibition, and declares the magnitude; the player of the payoff stands for the bearer of the norm; a norm is named at most once by each key; and every norm of C with a magnitude whose bearer is a player of the model is named, once the model names any. The size and the factor λ hold by definition, since the file states the magnitude once.

The shape of the answer. Let σ be a profile, D a design, and ℓ a parameter, with all other parameters fixed. Consider two conditions:

  • (i) ℓ occurs in no probability and in no reward value of the model, and every weight in which ℓ occurs is affine in ℓ (ℓ does not occur in it with a power above one).
  • (ii) for a player p: every term of p in whose weight ℓ occurs has a weight −m·ℓ with m ≥ 0, as sanction_of: terms have, and under σ the expected accumulated value of its reward structure is 0.

Proposition. Assume (i). Whether D is well-defined and whether σ is admissible do not depend on ℓ. Where they hold:

  1. Every gain_p is a convex, piecewise linear function of ℓ. On an interval of values of ℓ, the set of values at which σ is an ε-equilibrium of D is a closed interval, possibly empty. In particular, σ is an ε-equilibrium at every value between a and b if it is one at a and at b.
  2. If (ii) holds for p, gain_p is non-increasing in ℓ: if gain_p > ε at b, then gain_p > ε at every value below b.
  3. If (ii) holds for every player, the set of 1 is empty or consists of the values ℓ ≥ λ* of the interval, for a threshold λ*.

Proof. By (i), the probabilities, and with them the graph of the game and every expected accumulated reward, do not depend on ℓ, so neither do well-definedness and admissibility (H.5, I.6). For a strategy τ of p, the payoff of p under σ[p↦τ] is A(τ) + ℓ·B(τ) with numbers A(τ) and B(τ) that do not depend on ℓ, because every weight is affine in ℓ. BR_p is the maximum of finitely many such functions (I.7), hence convex and piecewise linear, and the payoff under σ is affine, so gain_p is convex and piecewise linear, and continuous. The set where a convex continuous function is at most ε is a closed interval, and so is the intersection over the players. Under (ii), B(τ) ≤ 0 for every τ, since it is minus a sum of magnitudes times expected accumulated rewards, and B is 0 for σ's own strategy; gain_p is then a maximum of non-increasing functions minus a constant. A closed interval that contains, with a value, every greater one is empty or of the form of 3. ∎

Where it stops.

  • Without (ii) the set is a closed interval and need not be a threshold set. In I.12, shortcut_first incurs the sanction, and it is an equilibrium exactly for max(0, T_1, T_2) ≤ λ ≤ T_3. A reward_of: term violates (ii) as well, since its value under σ grows with ℓ.
  • With two contracts there are two parameters bound to two lambda values. The proposition holds for each of them with the other fixed; it says nothing about a region of both.
  • When ℓ occurs in a probability (the incentive changes how the environment behaves), (i) fails, the payoffs are rational functions of ℓ, and nothing of the above holds.

Certification by the end points of a cell. The proposition is a special case of the proposition of the end-point certification of I.9, which is stated for any strategic model: condition (i) is condition A of I.9 for the weights that this appendix defines as well, and condition (ii) implies condition B− from the initial state, since a sanction_of: term has the coefficient −m of ℓ. I.9 turns it into a method for an equilibrium map whose only open parameter is ℓ: one exact evaluation at each end of a cell [a, b] certifies equilibrium, and one at b certifies a deviator for whom (ii) holds. I.9 also states the mirrored condition B+ (every coefficient of ℓ at least 0, with the expected value 0 under the profile), under which the gain is non-decreasing and the evaluation at a decides; a reward_of: term has a positive coefficient, but its expected value is positive under a profile that fulfils the norm with positive probability, and B+ then does not hold. J-14 says how the method applies to a file that declares this extension.

The example. The first model of I.12 is linked to a norm by declaring the two extensions, adding the norm to the contract, and replacing the weight of the third term of R:

sos:
  name: "RobotDeliveryGame"
  extensions:
    - stochastic: 0.1
    - games: 0.1
    - norms: 0.1
    - grounding: 0.1
  # actors, transitions as in I.12

  contracts:
    - id: DELIVERY
      parties: [DISPATCHER, "ROBOT[*]"]
      information: { alpha: 0.9 }
      authority:   { decision_holder: DISPATCHER, beta: 0.9 }
      incentives:  { lambda: 0.1 }
      norms:
        - id: no_shortcut
          kind: prohibition
          bearer: "ROBOT[i]"
          counterparty: DISPATCHER
          content: "ROBOT[i].handling == Careless"
          on_violation:
            severity: Major
            sanction: "deduction from the delivery fee"
            magnitude: 2

  model:
    # as in I.12, except the payoff of R:
    payoffs:
      - player: R
        until: "settled"
        terms:
          - { reward: fee,       weight: 10 }
          - { reward: effort,    weight: -1 }
          - { reward: shortcuts, sanction_of: "DELIVERY.no_shortcut" }
      - player: D
        until: "settled"
        terms:
          - { reward: fee, weight: 5 }

The weight of the third term is −2·lambda, the weight written by hand in I.12, so every number of I.12 is unchanged, and every result is reported with the assumption "the reward structure shortcuts is taken to count the violations of DELIVERY.no_shortcut". The bearer ROBOT[i] is covered by "ROBOT[*]", for which R stands (J-10). shortcuts counts the careless attempts of one run, up to three. Appendix F judges a norm once per contract instance, so the count agrees with the violations of the norm only if the host starts a contract instance for each attempt. That is an assumption about the host which nothing in the file expresses; it is the kind of assumption the report carries.

Conditions (i) and (ii) hold for comply: lambda occurs only in the weight of the term, which is −2·lambda, and comply takes no careless attempt, so the expected value of shortcuts under it is 0 (reported by J-12). With g the fee, c the cost of effort, s the probability that a careful attempt succeeds, and m the magnitude of no_shortcut, I.12 derives:

In each design with s·g ≥ c and s < 1, the compliance profile is a Nash equilibrium exactly when λ ≥ λ*, where λ* = max(0, (c − (s/2)·(g·(1 − s)² + c·(2 − s))) / m).

With g = 10, c = 1, and m = 2, λ* is 0.150108 in CENTRALIZED and 0.060373 in DISTRIBUTED at the fixed values. The declared λ = 0.1 makes compliance an equilibrium in DISTRIBUTED only. The threshold is inversely proportional to the magnitude. At λ = 0.1, compliance is an equilibrium in CENTRALIZED exactly when the magnitude is at least 480347064349/160000000000 (about 3.0022; 3.002 is not enough), and in DISTRIBUTED exactly when it is at least 193194567781/160000000000 (about 1.2075). For this contract χ = 1/1 = 1 (its one prohibition carries a sanction), α̂ is undefined (no items), and β̂ is undefined (no power): the coverage is complete, and the question that remains is the one the model answers, whether the size m·λ is enough.

J.8 Static semantics​

A processor that implements the extension MUST, in a file that declares grounding: 0.1, in addition to existing checks, apply the rules below. J-1 to J-4 and J-10 are errors; they concern only the keys of this appendix. Where two clauses would report the same fact about the same construct, a processor gives one diagnostic.

  • J-1. A file that declares grounding: 0.1 MUST also declare norms: 0.1; otherwise the processor reports an error and does not interpret the keys of this extension.
  • J-2. Every key of grounding.items MUST be an identifier and MUST NOT be a reserved word of A.11; its value MUST be an actor reference whose actor name is a party name of the contract. tolerance: MUST be a number in [0, 1].
  • J-3. shares: is allowed only for an obligation or a permission whose content: is a message_event. It MUST list at least one item, each declared in grounding.items of the same contract and each once. A processor SHOULD warn when the receiver of the message has the actor name of the origin of a listed item; when the target of a sharing entry that names a declared item, or the receiver of a sharing norm, is not a party of the contract or is a bare set name (J.4); when an entry of information.sharing that names a declared item is not of the form of J.4 with one item; and when an entry of information.sharing names an item that grounding.items does not declare while the contract declares items.
  • J-4. on_violation.magnitude and on_fulfilment.magnitude MUST be finite numbers that are not negative. on_fulfilment: is allowed only for an obligation or a prohibition (as on_violation:, N-4) and MUST have at least one of reward: and magnitude: that is not empty (J.3). A reward: MUST be a text (a scalar); a sequence or a mapping is an error. A processor SHOULD warn about a magnitude: of zero next to a sanction: or reward: text that is not empty.
  • J-5. For every contract of the file, a processor that implements the extension and reports MUST compute α̂, β̂, and χ by the definitions of J.4, exactly, and report each with its two counts, or as undefined with the reason, together with the further counts that J.4 names (the three ways of giving a pair, the member-specific entries and norms, the powers per bearer and the effects per power, the share of the decision holder, the norms with a reparation only and with a transition). For alpha and for beta, when declared and the index is defined, it MUST report the declared value, the index, and their difference. For lambda it reports the declared value next to χ and no difference. The comparisons of J-6 to J-9 and J-13 are exact; a declared value is the decimal that is written (H.2). The quantities are defined for a file without errors: for a file with an error of J-2 to J-4, no quantity is reported. For a file with two or more contracts, a processor that reports the quantities of all of them SHOULD also report α̂_SoS, β̂_SoS, and χ_SoS (J.4) with their counts; it compares them with nothing.
  • J-6. A processor SHOULD warn, and name a construct that is the counterexample, when a contract declares alpha: 0 and has a given pair, or has a non-empty information.sharing while α̂ is undefined; alpha: 1 and α̂ is defined and less than 1; beta: 1 and β̂ is defined and less than 1; or beta: 0 and β̂ is defined and greater than 1 divided by the number of distinct single actors that bear a power (J.5).
  • J-7. A processor SHOULD warn when the declared alpha or beta and the defined index differ by more than the tolerance of the contract and the declared value is neither 0 nor 1 (J-6 tests the end points, also where it gives no warning), and when the decision holder names a single actor whose share of the powers is smaller than β̂.
  • J-8. For a contract that has norms:, a processor SHOULD warn when the contract declares a lambda greater than zero and none of its obligations and prohibitions carries an incentive (J.4), and when it declares lambda: 0 and one of them does.
  • J-9. In a file that also declares stochastic: 0.1, for every design and every parameter bound to C.alpha or C.beta whose index is defined, a processor SHOULD emit an informational diagnostic when the fixed value of the parameter under the design differs from the index by more than the tolerance of C. It is not emitted for a design whose fixed value equals the declared value of a parameter that J-6 or J-7 already reported. A file without transitions: has the one design base.
  • J-10. sanction_of: and reward_of: are allowed only in a file that also declares stochastic: 0.1 and games: 0.1. A term MUST have at most one of weight:, sanction_of:, and reward_of:. The value MUST be a string C.n in which C is a declared contract and n an obligation or a prohibition of C that has an on_violation.magnitude (for sanction_of:) or an on_fulfilment.magnitude (for reward_of:) greater than zero. A model that has a term naming a norm of C MUST have exactly one parameter bound by from: "C.lambda". The player of the payoff MUST list, in actors:, a reference that covers the bearer of n: the two have the same actor name and do not have two different integer indices ("ROBOT[*]" and ROBOT[1] cover the bearer ROBOT[i]; ROBOT[1] does not cover the bearer ROBOT[2]). In one model, a norm MUST NOT be named by more than one sanction_of: term, nor by more than one reward_of: term.
  • J-11. When a model has a term that names a norm of a contract C, a processor SHOULD warn about every obligation or prohibition of C that has a magnitude greater than zero, whose bearer is covered by an actor reference of some player, and that no term names with the key that corresponds to that magnitude (a norm with both magnitudes needs both terms). It SHOULD also warn about a term with a weight: in which a parameter bound to C.lambda occurs: the size of such a term is not checked against a norm.
  • J-12. A processor that evaluates a payoff with a sanction_of: or reward_of: term MUST report, with every result that depends on the payoff, the assumption of J.7 for each such term. For every sanction_of: and every reward_of: term and every design it SHOULD report the expected accumulated value, until the target of the payoff, of the reward structure of the term under the compliance profile at the fixed values. A value greater than zero is information, not a warning: an obligation can be violated by chance although the bearer complies. For a sanction_of: term a value of zero is condition (ii) of J.7 for the compliance profile; a reward_of: term breaks (ii) whatever its value, by the sign of its weight.
  • J-13. A processor that compares two versions of a contract SHOULD report every change of an on_violation.magnitude, an on_fulfilment: block, a shares: list, and grounding.items, and SHOULD warn when, for α or for β, the declared value and the index are defined in both versions and (x′ − x)·(x̂′ − x̂) is less than zero, where x and x̂ are the declared value and the index of the old version and x′ and x̂′ those of the new one. When only one of the two versions declares the extension, the changes of these keys are reported against their absence, the processor says that the indices are not compared, and no direction warning is given. A norm that is new in the second version is listed with its magnitudes, on_fulfilment:, and shares:.
  • J-14. The certification by the end points of a cell of I.9 applies to an equilibrium map of a file that declares grounding: 0.1 as to any strategic model, with these precisions: a weight that J.7 defines (−m·ℓ or m′·ℓ) is a weight in the sense of its condition A, and ℓ is the open parameter of the query, whether or not it is bound to a C.lambda. Condition (ii) of J.7 for p implies condition B− of I.9 from the initial state; a processor MUST NOT claim that p is a deviator on a cell from the end points unless B− or B+ holds for p from s₀ (concept: nash) or from the deciding state s (concept: every_reachable_state), computed exactly at the values used for the other parameters. Up to cadl 0.3.23 this rule stated the method itself and required the declaration of grounding: 0.1; the method now belongs to I.9, and its claims in a file that declares this extension are those of I.9.

Rule A.4 that alpha, beta, and lambda lie in [0, 1] is unchanged, and so are the rules N-1 to N-12 and I-1 to I-11.

J.9 Example​

The contract below is that of F.8 with the three parameter blocks, a grounding: block, two shares: annotations, a magnitude, and one more power, decline, by which a robot refuses an assignment. The keys that F.8 already has are abridged.

sos:
  name: "RobotDeliveryNorms"
  extensions:
    - sos-dsl: 0.1
    - norms: 0.1
    - grounding: 0.1
  # actors DISPATCHER, ROBOT[1..N], CUSTOMER[1..M] as in F.8

  contracts:
    - id: DELIVERY_SLA
      parties: [DISPATCHER, "ROBOT[*]", "CUSTOMER[*]"]
      authority:   { decision_holder: DISPATCHER, beta: 0.9 }
      information:
        alpha: 0.6
        sharing:
          - "DISPATCHER -> ROBOT[*] : route"
          - "ROBOT[*] -> DISPATCHER : status"
          - "DISPATCHER -> CUSTOMER[*] : status"
      incentives:  { lambda: 0.5 }
      grounding:
        items:
          route:   DISPATCHER
          status:  "ROBOT[*]"
          battery: "ROBOT[*]"
          failure: "ROBOT[*]"
      # assume, guarantee, lifecycle, counts_as as in F.8

      norms:
        - id: acknowledge              # obligation of ROBOT[i], as in F.8
          on_violation: { severity: Major, reparation: report_failure }
        - id: report_failure           # obligation of ROBOT[i], as in F.8
          content: "ROBOT[i] -> DISPATCHER : failure_report"
          shares: [failure]
          on_violation: { severity: Critical, transition: Violated }
        - id: no_low_battery_driving   # prohibition of ROBOT[i], as in F.8
          on_violation:
            severity: Critical
            sanction: "removed from the dispatch pool for one period"
            magnitude: 4
        - id: may_return_to_charge     # permission of ROBOT[i], as in F.8
          content: "ROBOT[i] -> DISPATCHER : charging_request"
          shares: [battery]
        - id: reassign                 # power of DISPATCHER, as in F.8
        - id: decline
          kind: power
          bearer: "ROBOT[i]"
          content: "ROBOT[i] -> DISPATCHER : decline"
          effects:
            discharge: [acknowledge]

α̂. The party names are DISPATCHER, ROBOT, and CUSTOMER, and there are four items, each with two parties other than its origin: eight pairs. Every target denotes a whole party.

Item (origin)DISPATCHERROBOTCUSTOMER
route (DISPATCHER)origingiven by a sharing entrynot given
status (ROBOT)given by a sharing entryorigingiven by a sharing entry
battery (ROBOT)given by a permission (may_return_to_charge)originnot given
failure (ROBOT)given by an obligation (report_failure)originnot given

Three pairs are given by sharing entries, one by an obligation, and one by a permission; no entry is member-specific. Five pairs are given: α̂ = 5/8 = 0.625. The declared alpha: 0.6 differs by 0.025, within the tolerance. Had the first entry been "DISPATCHER -> ROBOT[3] : route", it would be member-specific, the pair (route, ROBOT) would not be given, and α̂ would be 4/8.

β̂. The contract has two powers. reassign is borne by DISPATCHER, a single actor; decline is borne by ROBOT[i], which is not a single actor. The largest count of a single actor is 1: β̂ = 1/2 = 0.5, and the share of the decision holder is also 1/2. reassign names two norms in its effects and decline one. The declared beta: 0.9 differs by 0.4, more than the tolerance of 0.25, and the processor warns (J-7): the contract declares a nearly centralised authority while half of its decision points lie with the robots. With beta: 1 the warning would be that of J-6 and would name decline.

χ. The contract has three obligations and prohibitions. no_low_battery_driving carries a sanction of magnitude 4. acknowledge has a reparation and no sanction, and report_failure has a lifecycle transition and no sanction; neither carries an incentive. χ = 1/3 = 0.333333, with one norm that has a reparation only and one that has a transition. The declared lambda: 0.5 is greater than zero and a norm carries an incentive, so J-8 gives no warning.

By the definition, an amendment that removes decline raises β̂ to 1/1 = 1, and by Lemma 3 one that gives report_failure a sanction raises χ to 2/3.

J.10 Intermediate Representation (IR) addition​

Each contract under institution.contracts gains the field grounding, and each norm of F.9 four fields. For the example of J.9:

{
  "grounding": {
    "items": { "route": "DISPATCHER", "status": "ROBOT[*]", "battery": "ROBOT[*]", "failure": "ROBOT[*]" },
    "tolerance": 0.25,
    "indices": {
      "alpha":    { "given": 5, "pairs": 8,
                    "by_sharing": 3, "by_obligation": 1, "by_permission": 1, "member_specific": 0 },
      "beta":     { "largest": 1, "powers": 2, "holder": 1,
                    "bearers": { "DISPATCHER": 1, "ROBOT[i]": 1 } },
      "coverage": { "incentive": 1, "norms": 3, "reparation_only": 1, "transition": 1 }
    }
  },
  "norms": [
    { "id": "no_low_battery_driving",
      // ... the fields of F.9
      "shares": [],
      "on_violation_magnitude": 4,
      "on_fulfilment_reward": null,
      "on_fulfilment_magnitude": null }
  ]
}

The field grounding and the four norm fields are absent unless the extension is declared (with norms: 0.1); the IR of every other file is unchanged. items is an empty mapping and tolerance the default when the contract has no grounding: block. alpha, beta, or coverage is null when the quantity is undefined; otherwise the first two fields are its numerator and denominator, so that the exact ratio can be formed. by_sharing, by_obligation, and by_permission count pairs and may overlap; member_specific counts entries and norms, each once, and does not count bare set names. holder is the count k(h) of the decision holder, or null when it does not name a single actor; bearers maps each bearer reference, as written, to its number of powers. Magnitudes are null when absent, and an empty text is null.

For a file with two or more contracts, the institution layer gains the field grounding with the quantities of the SoS (J.4), in the form of the indices of a contract:

{
  "institution": {
    "actors": [ /* ... */ ],
    "contracts": [ /* ... */ ],
    "grounding": {
      "contracts": ["C1", "C2"],
      "indices": {
        "alpha":    { "given": 1, "pairs": 3, "by_sharing": 1, "by_obligation": 0,
                      "by_permission": 0, "member_specific": 0 },
        "beta":     { "largest": 2, "powers": 3, "bearers": { "D": 2, "X": 1 } },
        "coverage": { "incentive": 2, "norms": 3, "reparation_only": 1, "transition": 0 }
      }
    }
  }
}

contracts lists the ids of the contracts, sorted. bearers sums the powers of each bearer reference over all contracts, sorted by the reference; beta has no holder. The field is absent when the extension is not declared or the file has fewer than two contracts.

A payoff term of the IR of I.13 gains the fields sanction_of and reward_of (a string C.n, or null), written for every term when the extension is declared (both null for a term with a written weight). For a term with one of them, weight is the defined weight as text: -m * p or m * p, with m the magnitude written as a plain decimal without exponent (as the numbers of H.11) and p the id of the parameter bound to C.lambda ("-2 * lambda" in J.7). Diagnostics are not in the IR.

J.11 Reference implementation status​

The reference implementation implements grounding 0.1 from cadl 0.3.23 as follows. Up to 0.3.22, the extension name was unknown and its keys were ignored without a diagnostic.

  • cadl check applies the rules J-1 to J-9, and in a strategic model J-10 to J-12 (the SHOULDs as warnings, J-9 and J-12 as informational diagnostics), and lowers the keys to the IR of J.10. An undeclared use gets one warning per file (J.12). A key that this appendix does not define inside grounding: or on_fulfilment: (a typo such as tolerence:) gets a warning; a typo of magnitude: inside on_violation: or of shares: cannot be told from an unknown key of the core language and is silent.
  • cadl norms-indices FILE [--contract ID] [--format text|json] reports the quantities of J.4 with all their counts, the comparisons of J.5, and the diagnostics of J-9 per design. From cadl 0.3.24, for a file with two or more contracts and without --contract, it also reports the quantities of the SoS (J.4), in a last section of the text and under the key sos of the JSON output, and cadl sim-ir writes them into the IR (J.10). The output for each contract is unchanged. It refuses a file with an error, in particular of J-2 to J-4 (exit status 2); warnings do not change the exit status 0.
  • cadl norms-diff reports the findings of J-13, including a version that does not declare the extension. By default it compares the contracts that have norms: or counts_as:; a contract without norms whose grounding.items change is compared with --contract.
  • The keys are kept apart from the norms read by Appendix F, so cadl norms-eval, cadl agents-run, the boundary decision, and the generated monitor give the same verdicts with and without them. The generated monitor of an annotated file is identical to that of the file without the keys only when every annotated norm already had an on_violation: block: adding on_violation: { magnitude: m } to a norm without one creates the block, and the monitor then carries the default severity, with the same behaviour.
  • The core parser reads alpha, beta, and lambda as binary floating-point numbers; the shortest decimal that round-trips is taken as the written decimal (J-5), so a value with more than 17 significant digits is not recovered. A reference without an index to an undeclared actor counts as a single actor (other rules report it), and bearers is keyed by the reference without white space.
  • With Appendix I (cadl game-check), the link of J.7 is implemented: a sanction_of: or reward_of: term gets the weight of J.7, every check and map result carries the assumption of J.7, and J-14 is used in --certify when the conditions of I.9 hold (the map reports whether condition A holds and why not, and the players for which the gain is non-increasing or non-decreasing; cells certified from the end points carry method: "end points (I.9)", in 0.3.23 "endpoints (J-14)"). Since the method belongs to I.9, a file without grounding: 0.1 uses it as well (I.14). With the link implemented, the status not_evaluated of I.13 cannot arise for a valid file: an unusable reference is an error of J-1 or J-10.
  • Verification of the indices: an independent review compared the quantities with two scripts on 4 300 and 2 000 random contracts, with no disagreement. The invariant of J.12 held on 4 454 random files and on 174 sources of the test suite: adding - grounding: 0.1 to a file that declares norms: 0.1 gained no error. The lemmas of J.4 were tested as properties on seeded random contracts. The verdicts of cadl norms-eval and cadl agents-run on the bundled traces were identical with and without magnitudes and on_fulfilment: on every norm, and the outputs of the 17 bundled examples of 0.3.22 (331 files) were identical to 0.3.22.
  • Verification of the link: an independent review ran 840 generated models through the programming interface with the bounds of I.9 disabled, so that every decided cell came from J-14 (12 416 point evaluations), and 320 through the command line (3 496 point evaluations), against an oracle that enumerates all strategies: no wrong claim. The implementer's fuzzing certified cells by J-14 on 1 200 models, among them games with ℓ in a probability or in a weight that is not affine, which J-14 refused (50 733 point evaluations, no wrong claim), and detected injected errors that drop conditions 2 and 5.
  • The example of J.7 gives every result of the first example of I.12 with the written weight; J-12 reports 0 in both designs. The least magnitudes at λ = 0.1 are reproduced exactly: with magnitude: 3.00216915218125 (480347064349/160000000000) comply_nash is an equilibrium in CENTRALIZED and with 3.00216915218124 it is not; with 1.20746604863125 (193194567781/160000000000) it is one in DISTRIBUTED and with 1.20746604863124 it is not. A value written in a file has at most 15 significant digits (H.2), so the threshold is tested with these two neighbours.
  • The quantities of J.9 are those printed by cadl norms-indices for the example, with one warning (J-7 for beta).
  • Verification of the quantities of the SoS: hand-computed files with two and three contracts, the counterexamples of J.4, and, on seeded random files of two to four contracts, Lemma 4, the lemmas that carry over, the bounds when a contract is added, and invariance under reordering the contracts.

J.12 Backward compatibility​

Files written against CADL v0.3 without this extension remain valid and are read the same way, and their IR does not change (J.10). The extension changes no verdict of Appendix F and no result of Appendices H and I: a term with sanction_of: is a term of I.6 with a defined weight.

Declaring the extension never invalidates a file that declares norms: 0.1. A file that declares norms: 0.1, is valid under Appendix F, and uses none of the keys of this appendix remains valid when grounding: 0.1 is added to its extensions:. Of the rules that are errors, J-1 cannot fire for such a file, and J-2, J-3, J-4, and J-10 apply only to the keys of this appendix; every comparison of a declared parameter with an index (J-6 to J-9) is a warning or an informational diagnostic.

A file that uses grounding:, shares:, on_fulfilment:, on_violation.magnitude, sanction_of:, or reward_of: without declaring grounding: 0.1 does not conform to this appendix. A processor that implements the extension does not interpret these keys in such a file and SHOULD report them as undeclared extension keys, with one warning per file that lists the keys used (a key whose value is null is not used): no quantity is computed, and a payoff term with sanction_of: or reward_of: is an error by I-7. A file that declares grounding: 0.1 without norms: 0.1 gets the error of J-1.

A processor that implements Appendix I and not this appendix MUST NOT evaluate, in a file that declares both, a payoff that has a sanction_of: or reward_of: term, nor a query that needs it; it reports the query as not evaluated (I-7). In no case is such a term read as a term with the default weight. Processors that do not implement the extension MUST NOT reject files that use it; they SHOULD emit a single informational diagnostic per file.
