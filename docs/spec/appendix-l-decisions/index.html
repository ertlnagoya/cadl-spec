Appendix L — Decision Records (decisions 0.3)

Appendix K, K.5 lets a version of a model name the records of decisions on which it rests, by a URI reference, and says nothing about the records themselves. This appendix gives a format for such a record: a decision record. With it, a tool that does not know the system that wrote a record can read it, check it, and show it.

A decision record is a file beside the model, not a key of the model. A CADL file declares nothing to use it, and no verdict of the core or of an extension depends on it. The format is normative; whether a system keeps a decision record is its own choice. L.10 says what the reference implementation and the exercise of Course D do with it.

L.1 Motivation and scope​

The robot town of Course D amends its rules after every period. An LLM proposes amendments, a tool measures them on the same random seeds and applies the gates of the constitution, the three companies vote, and when the gates or the LLM say so, a human decides after reading a decision request in which the LLM recommends an option. The human also writes the KPI, and chooses how it is measured when the LLM reads it in more than one way. Changes of the regulation, the values of the constitution that bound the loop, are decided by another person: the regulator.

Two questions are asked of such a loop afterwards.

  • Consensus. Who agreed to each change? Did the companies vote for it, and if not, who set their vote aside?
  • Accountability. Who decided, by which procedure, on what basis, and why? What did the deciders not know, and what did they promise to check later?

The exercise answers them in decisions.md and gsn.yaml, which are written for people, and, after robot-town v0.2.2, in evidence.json, which a program can read but which is tied to the exercise: the names of its gates and metrics are those of the simulator. A decision record answers the same questions in a form that does not depend on the system. For each decision it says:

  • under which objective it was taken (the text that the owner wrote, and how it is measured);
  • what was proposed, by whom, and what was measured;
  • which checks each proposal passed, and who made them;
  • how each party voted;
  • what each proposer predicted and what an advisor recommended, with their reasons, assumptions, and what they said they did not know;
  • who decided, by which procedure, with which reason, assumptions and unknowns, and on which earlier entries the decision rests;
  • which version of the model came into force;
  • what the deciders promised to follow up, what would fulfil each promise, and whether it was done.

The same evidence is asked of every party. A proposal, a recommendation, or a decision made by an LLM or by a tool is recorded with the same fields, and checked by the same rules, as one made by a person: its reason, the entries it rests on, its assumptions, what it predicts, and what it does not know (L.4, "Evidence fields"). In Course D the LLM proposes and recommends, and it may also decide in place of the human, for amendments of the rules and of the contract with the customers, and in place of the regulator for the regulation. A reviewer must be able to read a decision of the LLM as closely as a decision of the human, and to tell from the record who decided in whose place and on which layers.

In scope: the entries and their fields, the references between them, the integrity of the file, and the checks of a verifier. Out of scope: the procedure that produces the entries (which gates a system applies, how proposals are made), the meaning of a metric (its formula and its measurement method are in the model, K.5), and how a tool shows a record.

L.2 Concepts​

  • A decision record is one file. It covers one series of decisions on one model: a run of an amendment loop, or the life of a system in operation.

  • A party is a person, an organisation, a software agent, an LLM, or a tool that takes part in the decisions. Each party has one or more roles:

    RoleWhat the party does
    ownerwrites the objective
    proposerproposes amendments, or readings of an objective
    assessormeasures proposals and checks them
    votervotes on proposals
    advisorrecommends an option to a decider
    deciderdecides, on the layers given to it (layers, L.3), also in place of another party (on_behalf_of, L.3)
    affectedis affected by a decision and takes no part in it (for example customers); named so that an entry can say who is affected

  • An entry is one line of the record after its header. Each entry has a type (L.4), an identifier, the party that made it, the point in the time of the system to which it belongs (a period or an instant, as in K.4), and the time at which it was written.

  • The basis of an entry is the list of earlier entries on which it rests. A decision names its basis: the objective in force, the measurements, the checks, the votes, the recommendation.

  • A follow-up is a promise, made with a decision, to check or do something later. A later entry fulfils it. A follow-up can say what a fulfilling entry must be: for a promise to measure again, a measurement under the same conditions as the one it re-checks.

  • A delegation is an entry by which a party lets another party decide in its place: on which layers, by which procedures, under which conditions, and until when. A tool that adopts changes by its own rule and an LLM that answers decision requests in place of a person are delegates alike. A decision made within a delegation in force is shown under it; a decision made in another's place without one is reported for review.

  • Entries are never changed. A correction is a later entry that says what was wrong in an earlier one.

L.3 File format​

A decision record is a file of JSON Lines in UTF-8, each line ended by a line feed. The first line is a header, then come the entries in the order in which they were written, and a complete record ends with an end record. The suggested file name is decisions.jsonl.

Every line ends with the three integrity fields seq, prev, and digest of Appendix G, G.8, computed as G.8 says: over the canonical JSON of the line, with SHA-256 or, for a keyed record, HMAC-SHA256. A line is in the written form of G.8. What G.8 says about the limits of the chain and about an anchor kept outside the file holds for a decision record as well: a writer SHOULD keep the digest of the last line and the run_id outside the record.

LineFields before the integrity fields
Headerdecisions_format: "cadl-decisions/3". hash, key_id, run_id: as in the header of G.8. model: name (the sos.name of the model), version (the sos.version of the first version, or null), source_sha256 (the SHA-256 of the bytes of the first version of the CADL file, or null). time_base: what a period is, in words, or null. started: the time at which the record was started, as an ISO 8601 time stamp in UTC. previous: for a record that continues another, the run_id of that record and the digest of its end record; otherwise null. parties: the parties (below).
EntryThe common fields of L.4, then the fields of its type.
End recorddecisions_end: true. entries: the number of entries between the header and the end record.

A record without end record is still being written, or its writer did not finish; a verifier reports it as open. A writer that adds entries to a complete record writes them after a new header: a record has exactly one header, so a writer that continues a series of decisions starts a new record, whose header names the previous record in previous (its run_id and the digest of its end record).

Parties. Each party in parties has these fields:

FieldContent
idIdentifier of the party in the record: letters, digits, _, -, ..
kindhuman, organisation, agent, llm, or tool.
rolesOne or more roles of L.2.
nameA name for a reader, or null.
actorThe actor id or the role of the model that the party is, as decided_by: and voters: of K.3 name it (COUNCIL, service_operator), or null.
modelFor an llm, the model identifier; for a tool, its name and version; otherwise null.
on_behalf_ofThe id of the party in whose place this party acts, or null. An LLM that decides in place of the service operator has the role decider itself and names the operator's party here.
layersThe layers on which the party decides: a list of rule, contract, and regulation (the layers of K.3), or null when they are not limited. For a party that acts on behalf of another, the layers on which it does so.

layers separates who may decide what. In Course D the service operator decides on the rules and the contract (["rule", "contract"]), the regulator on the regulation (["regulation"]), and the steward, a tool, adopts by itself only changes of the rules (["rule"]); an LLM that decides in place of the operator has at most the operator's layers.

A party whose kind is human stands for a role held by a person; the record does not need to name the person. Who held the role is a matter for the organisation that keeps the record. A party of kind llm or tool gives model, so that a reader knows which model or which version of a tool made its entries.

L.4 Entries​

Common fields​

FieldContent
idIdentifier of the entry, unique in the record: letters, digits, _, -, .. It is the fragment by which K.5 names the entry (L.7).
typeOne of the types below.
pointThe point of the system's time to which the entry belongs: {"period": k} (k ≥ 1) or {"at": "<ISO 8601>"}, as the time_point of K.4.
writtenThe time at which the entry was written, an ISO 8601 time stamp in UTC.
byThe id of the party that made the entry.
basisThe ids of earlier entries on which this entry rests; may be empty.
fulfilsThe ids of earlier follow_up entries that this entry fulfils; may be empty.
attachmentsFiles that belong to the entry: path (relative to the record), sha256, media_type or null. For example the table of a measurement, the output of an LLM, the CADL file of a proposal.
generatedFor an entry whose text an LLM produced, also when another party wrote the entry: model (the model identifier) and request_digest (a digest of the request to the LLM, so that two runs with the same input can be told apart from two with different input). Otherwise null.

An option of a decision, a request, or a recommendation is the id of a proposal entry, or "keep" for leaving the model as it is.

Evidence fields​

A proposal, a recommendation, and a decision carry four more fields, whatever the kind of the party that makes them:

FieldContent
reasonWhy the party proposes, recommends, or decides it, in the party's own words. Not empty, except in a decision with procedure automatic, which gives rule instead and may give null.
assumptionsWhat the party takes to be true without having checked it: a list of texts.
unknownsWhat the party says it does not know and would need to know: a list of texts.
predictionsWhat the party expects to happen: a list of option (the option the prediction is about; for a proposal, its own id), subject (metric:<name> for a metric, metric:<name>@<party> for a metric of one party, or vote:<party> for a vote), expected (increase, decrease, unchanged, yes, no, or a condition as text, such as "<= 1"), and note (text or null).

A list may be empty: an empty unknowns says that the party names nothing it does not know. The fields let a reviewer compare what each party said with what was measured afterwards, in the same way for a person and for an LLM.

Types​

objective: the text of an objective as its owner wrote it (the KPI of Course D), in force from point until the next objective.

FieldContent
textThe text, as written.
measureHow the objective is measured, when the owner wrote it: a list of measure items (below). null when it is to be read by a proposer; then proposal entries of kind measure offer readings and a decision chooses one.

A measure item has metric (a metric of the model, or a name of the system), role (goal, watch, or guard), direction (increase, decrease, or null), and target (a condition as text, such as "<= 1", or null). The first goal is the main goal.

proposal: a proposed change.

FieldContent
kindamendment (a new version of the model) or measure (a reading of an objective).
titleA short title.
changesFor an amendment: a list of parameter (the id of a parameter of K.3, or a name of the system), from, to (numbers or durations as text), and layer (rule, contract, regulation, or null).
objectiveFor a reading: the id of the objective it reads.
measureFor a reading: the measure items.
affectedThe ids of parties that the change affects and that take no part in deciding it, such as customers for a change of the contract with them; may be empty.

A proposal also gives the evidence fields.

measurement: values measured for the version in force or for a proposal.

FieldContent
of"in_force", or the id of a proposal.
versionThe sos.version of the version measured (for in_force), or null.
methodruns (number of runs), window (duration as text, or null), and description (text, or null), as measure: of K.5.
valuesThe measured values, a map from metric name to number, combined over the runs.
per_partyValues per party: a map from party id to a map from metric name to number, or null. For example the utility of each company.
compared_withThe id of the measurement this one is compared with, or null.
runs_betterOf the runs, how many are better than in compared_with on the main goal, or null.

check: the results of checking one proposal.

FieldContent
proposalThe id of the proposal.
resultsA list of name (the name of the check in the system, such as cadl check, constitution, invariants, gain), passed (true, false, or null when not applicable), and note (text or null).

vote: the vote of one voter on one proposal.

FieldContent
proposalThe id of the proposal.
valueyes, no, or abstain.
reasonThe reason the voter gave, or null.

request: a request for a decision, sent to a decider.

FieldContent
toThe id of the decider.
questionThe question.
optionsThe options.
whyWhy the decision is asked of the decider: a list of option and reason (text).

recommendation: what an advisor recommends.

FieldContent
requestThe id of the request, or null.
optionsA list of option, summary, and affected (who gains and who loses, as text).
recommendsThe recommended option, or null.

A recommendation also gives the evidence fields.

decision: a decision.

FieldContent
questionThe question decided, or null.
optionsThe options that were open.
choiceThe option chosen.
proceduresingle (the decider decided alone), vote (the decision follows the votes on choice), override (the decider set aside the outcome of the votes), or automatic (a tool applied a rule stated in rule).
ruleFor automatic, the rule in words (for example "adopt the proposal with the largest gain among those that pass every check and the vote"); otherwise null.
affectedThe ids of parties affected by the decision, or empty.
resultThe sos.version in force after the decision, or null for a decision on a reading.

A decision also gives the evidence fields.

version: a version of the model comes into force.

FieldContent
versionIts sos.version.
previousThe sos.version it replaces, or null.
effectiveWhen it takes effect, as effective: of K.4.
source_sha256The SHA-256 of the bytes of its CADL file.
decisionThe id of the decision that adopted it, or null for the first version.

follow_up: a promise to check or to do something later.

FieldContent
textWhat is to be done.
dueBy when, as a point, or null.
decisionThe id of the decision with which it was made, or null.
fulfilled_byWhat fulfils it, or null for any later entry that names it in fulfils. An object with type (the type of the entry, or null), and, for a measurement, of ("in_force" or the id of a proposal, or null), same_method_as (the id of an earlier measurement whose method the fulfilling measurement must have: the same runs, window and description, that is, the same conditions; or null), and subjects (the values it must give, as metric:<name> or metric:<name>@<party>; may be empty).

A promise to re-measure names the measurement it re-checks in same_method_as: a measurement under other conditions (other seeds, another demand) does not fulfil it, however close its values. A promise to act (to propose a mitigation, to revise a reading) gives type decision or proposal.

delegation: a party lets another party decide in its place.

FieldContent
toThe id of the party that decides in place of by: a party whose on_behalf_of is by.
layersThe layers on which to may decide in place of by: a list of rule, contract, and regulation, or null for every layer of to. An empty list ends the delegation.
proceduresThe procedures of decision that to may use: a list of single, vote, override, and automatic, or null for any.
conditionsThe conditions under which to decides, in words (for example, the checks that a proposal must pass before a tool adopts it); may be empty. A verifier does not check them.
untilThe last point that it covers, as a point, or null.
reasonWhy by delegates.

A delegation is in force from its point to its until, and until a later delegation from the same party to the same party replaces it. Whether a party may delegate, and to whom, is for the organisation that keeps the record; the record shows who delegated, when, and within which limits, so that a reader can tell a decision that a delegate made within them from one that it made outside them.

correction: a correction of an earlier entry.

FieldContent
correctsThe id of the entry.
textWhat was wrong, and what is right.

note: anything else a party wants on the record, as text.

L.5 References and order​

An entry refers only to entries written before it: the ids in basis, fulfils, proposal, of, compared_with, request, decision, objective, corrects, options, and choice are ids of earlier lines (or "keep"); the to of a delegation is the id of a party. The point of the entries does not decrease. Within one point, the entries are written in the order in which they arise: delegations, objective, proposals, measurements and checks, votes, request, recommendation, decision, follow-ups, version. This order is a recommendation: a verifier does not report a record that departs from it.

L.6 Checks and findings​

A verifier MUST report the following as errors. A record with an error cannot be relied on as it stands.

  • L-1 The file is JSON Lines in UTF-8, the first line is a header with decisions_format "cadl-decisions/3", and every line has the fields of L.3 and L.4 for its kind and type, with no others (for a record of "cadl-decisions/1" or "cadl-decisions/2", see L.11).
  • L-2 The integrity fields verify as in G.8, with the key when the record is keyed.
  • L-3 The ids of the parties are unique, and so are the ids of the entries.
  • L-4 Every id that an entry names is the id of an earlier entry of the type that the field requires (L.5), or "keep" where an option is meant; fulfils names follow_up entries; point does not decrease.
  • L-5 by names a party with the role that the type requires: objective owner; proposal proposer; measurement and check assessor; vote voter; recommendation advisor; decision and delegation decider; request, follow_up, version, correction, and note any party. A decision that adopts a proposal of kind amendment is made by a party whose layers is null or holds the layer of every change of the proposal.
  • L-6 The choice of a decision is one of its options. The votes on a proposal are the vote entries on it, one per voter: a later vote of a voter replaces an earlier one. A decision with procedure vote or override decides on choice, or, for a choice of "keep", on the first proposal among its options; it has in its basis every earlier vote on that proposal. With vote, the votes decide as the authority of K.3 says: the yes votes of its voters reach the quorum exactly when the decision adopts the proposal. With override, they do not. Whether the votes reach the quorum can be checked only with the CADL file of the version in force (L-9 to L-11); without it, a verifier says that it did not check it. A decision with procedure automatic gives rule, and its decider is a party of kind tool; any other decision gives rule as null.
  • L-7 A version entry that adopts a proposal names the decision whose choice is that proposal, and that decision's result is the version's version.
  • L-8 Every proposal, recommendation, and decision gives the evidence fields of L.4, whatever the kind of its party, and its reason is not empty except as L.4 allows for automatic. A party of kind llm or tool gives model. The on_behalf_of of a party names another party, and no party acts in its own place. The subject of a prediction names a metric or a party with the role voter. A party that acts on behalf of another acts only on layers that the other decides: when the other gives layers, its own layers are not null and are among them. The to of a delegation is a party whose on_behalf_of is the delegation's by, and the delegation's layers, when to gives layers, are not null and are among them.

When the CADL files of the versions are at hand (the paths in the attachments of the version entries, or given to the verifier), a verifier MUST also report:

  • L-9 The source_sha256 of the header and of each version entry is the SHA-256 of the file, and its sos.version, amendment.previous and amendment.effective are those of the entry.
  • L-10 Each parameter of a proposal of kind amendment that a decision adopts is a parameter of K.3 of the version in force, its from is the value of the parameter, and its to is a value that K.3 allows in one amendment. A proposal that no decision adopts may lie outside them: the record shows what was proposed, and its checks show that it was turned down; a verifier reports it as not amendable.
  • L-11 The decider of a decision that adopts a proposal is the authority of K.3 for each parameter it changes (the party whose actor is decided_by:), or acts on behalf of that party (on_behalf_of), or the decision has procedure override. The voters whose votes decide are parties whose actor is in voters:.

A verifier also reports the following findings. A finding is not an error: the record shows something that a reviewer should look at, and whether it was right is for the reviewer to judge.

FindingWhat the record shows
overrideA decision set aside the outcome of the votes (procedure override).
against recommendationA decision chose another option than the recommendation in its basis.
delegatedA decision was made by a party that acts on behalf of another (on_behalf_of), for example an LLM in place of the service operator, and no delegation in force covers it: none from that other party to it whose layers hold the layer of every change of the proposal that the decision adopts, and whose procedures hold its procedure. A verifier names, for each decision that a delegation covers, that delegation, and does not report it.
unknownsA decision, or a recommendation in its basis, lists what its party did not know.
prediction missedA prediction of a proposal, a recommendation, or a decision is contradicted by a later entry. For a metric: a later measurement of the option it is about, compared with the measurement in its compared_with, that is not above it (increase), not below it (decrease), different from it (unchanged, within a tolerance that the verifier states), or that does not meet the condition. For a vote: a later vote of the party it names, other than the one expected.
affected not measuredA decision adopts a proposal whose affected parties, or, for a change of a parameter of layer contract, the parties with the role affected, have no values in per_party of any measurement of the proposal in the basis.
unmeasured choiceA decision adopts a proposal of kind amendment with no measurement of it in its basis.
reading not decidedAn objective without measure was followed by measurements before any decision chose a reading.
fulfilment not shownAn entry names a follow-up in fulfils but is not what its fulfilled_by asks: another type, a measurement of something else, by another method than the measurement named in same_method_as, or without a value of a subject. Such an entry does not fulfil the follow-up.
open follow-upA follow-up has not been fulfilled (no later entry names it in fulfils and meets its fulfilled_by), and its due point has passed (or the record is complete).
not amendableA proposal of kind amendment that no decision adopts changes a parameter that K.3 of the version in force does not declare, or to a value that K.3 does not allow in one amendment (L-10).
correctedAn entry has a correction.
open recordThe record has no end record.

L.7 Relation to Appendix K​

An entry of amendment.decisions (K.5) names an entry of a decision record with kind: record and a URI reference whose fragment is the id of the entry:

sos:
  version: v2
  amendment:
    previous: v1
    effective: { period: 2 }
    decisions:
      - id: d1
        kind: record
        ref: decisions.jsonl#D1
        text: "block_k 3 → 2, adopted against the votes of B and C"
    parameters:
      - id: block_k
        layer: rule
        value: 2
        decision: d1
        # ...

The record and the model are written by different parties and at different times, so neither depends on the other: a model may name entries of a record, and a record names versions of the model by sos.version and by the SHA-256 of their files (L-9).

L.8 Mapping to GSN (informative)​

A decision record can be shown as a GSN assurance case. A tool that derives the GSN from the record, instead of writing it separately, keeps the two from disagreeing.

a top goal of the series, such as "the system keeps meeting the objectives of its owner"Goal (written by the tool)

Here the verifier reports unknowns, and names G.llm-decider as the delegation under which D1 was made. Whether the LLM may decide in place of the operator, and for which layers, is for the organisation that keeps the record; the record shows that it did, on which layers, and who let it. Without G.llm-decider, or had the LLM set aside the outcome of the votes (override, which G.llm-decider does not give), the verifier would report delegated. An LLM that acts for the operator cannot adopt a change of the regulation: the operator's layers do not hold it (L-8), and a decision that adopts such a change is an error (L-5). A change of the regulation goes to the regulator in a request of its own, and an LLM that decides it acts on behalf of the regulator.

L.10 Reference implementation status​

From cadl 0.3.39 (decisions 0.3; cadl 0.3.38 implemented decisions 0.2, and 0.3.37 decisions 0.1):

  • cadl.decisions.DecisionChain writes a record, and cadl decisions-verify RECORD.jsonl checks it: the errors L-1 to L-8, and L-9 to L-11 against the CADL files of the versions (the .cadl attachments of the version entries and the files given with --version). It reports the findings of L.6 and lists what it could not check. It counts a value as unchanged within 1 % of the value it is compared with. --key, --run-id and --anchor work as for cadl audit-verify. It reads records of cadl-decisions/1 and /2 as well (L.11). The writer fills layers and fulfilled_by with null when its caller leaves them out. For each decision made within a delegation it names the delegation.
  • cadl-robot-town (from v0.4.0) writes decisions.jsonl in each run of town.steward and town.council. The operator decides on the rules and the contract, and a regulator, another party, on the regulation, in a decision request of its own. With --decider llm, the LLM answers the requests in place of the human for the layers given with --delegate, as a party with on_behalf_of. A promise to re-measure names the measurement of its period in same_method_as, and the steward makes that measurement in the next period. From v0.5.0 each version entry, the first version included, attaches the CADL file of its version, written with the amendment extension (Appendix K: the amendable parameters, their places and ranges, and who decides each layer), so the verifier checks L-9 to L-11 and the outcome of the votes. The steward and the council adopt rule changes on behalf of the operator; a regulation proposal is measured by applying its value to the measurements and votes of its period; a follow-up says whether a measurement, a proposal or a decision fulfils it. From v0.6.0 the record starts with the delegations of the operator (and of the regulator): to the steward or the council the rule layer by its own rule (automatic), and to the LLM the layers given with --delegate. A regulation proposal is also applied to every proposal measured in the earlier periods, and for each company the measurement gives how many proposals would pass although it voted against them. A question promised in a follow-up is put to its decider when it falls due, and every open question in the last period. python -m town.check_examples runs the examples again in continuous integration and compares their histories, GSN and findings.
  • The page Decision Evidence of cadl-explorer (from v1.5.0) reads a record, verifies it with cadl.decisions, and shows a timeline of the parties, the trail of each decision, the evidence of each party, the GSN of L.8, the findings and the chain. From v1.6.0 it shows the layers of each decider and whether each follow-up was fulfilled. From v1.7.0 it takes the CADL files of the versions with the record, and checks L-9 to L-11 with them. From v1.8.0 it lists the delegations and the decisions made within each, and shows them in the timeline and the GSN.

L.11 Backward compatibility​

The format is new. It changes no key of a CADL file and no verdict. K.5 is unchanged; L.7 says how a ref: of K.5 names an entry of a decision record.

decisions 0.3 adds the entry type delegation to decisions 0.2, and the header names "cadl-decisions/3". It also narrows L-10 to the proposals that a decision adopts, for records of every format: a proposal outside K.3 that no decision adopts was an error and is now the finding not amendable. A verifier reads a record of "cadl-decisions/2" as before: it has no delegations, so every decision made on behalf of another party is reported as delegated, and a delegation entry in it is an error (L-1).

decisions 0.2 adds two fields to decisions 0.1: layers of a party and fulfilled_by of a follow-up, and the header names "cadl-decisions/2". A record of "cadl-decisions/1" has neither field; a verifier reads it as a record in which every party decides on any layer and every follow-up is fulfilled by any later entry that names it, and does not accept the two fields in it.
