9. Research Directions and Open Challenges

Based on use case analysis in Chapter 8 and a survey of existing research, we organize research directions toward CADL realization and development. For each direction, we state what the current state of the art already provides (named tools and results), what remains open for CADL, and the concrete research challenges that CADL should address. Where an existing tool already solves part of a problem, we say which part CADL would hand to that tool and which question the tool cannot answer.

The starting point is this specification (Version 0.3) and the reference implementation cadl 0.3.22. It checks the satisfiability of contracts with Z3 [de Moura & Bjørner, 2008], detects protocol deadlocks by structural analysis, supports the SoS-DSL extension (Appendix E: lifecycle:, monitors:, deadline:), generates Python, Solidity, OPA/Rego, and Unity C# code, drafts CADL from natural language with cadl ai (a parse / type-check / retry loop, and with --verify the checks of cadl verify), and reports a mapping to IEC 62853 (Chapter 10).

Statements below that no work addresses a given problem are based on a limited literature search (mainly 2023–2026). They mean that we did not find such work, not that none exists.

9.1 Algebraic Composition Theory for Institutions​

Establishment of an algebraic foundation for safely composing and decomposing multiple institutional descriptions is necessary. Benveniste et al. [Foundations and Trends in EDA, 2018] theorized parallel composition of contracts C₁ ⊗ C₂ in component design. Since then the algebra of assume-guarantee contracts has been largely completed: hypercontracts give closed forms for composition and quotient (the specification of a missing component) over hyperproperties [Incer+, NFM 2022], and Pacti computes the algebraic operations of contract theory efficiently [Incer+, arXiv 2023]. OCRA checks the refinement of contracts written in LTL, including metric real-time constraints [Cimatti+, ASE 2013]; Kind 2 checks that the modes of a contract cover its assumptions [Champion+, CAV 2016]; and contracts have been composed across agents with quantitative temporal requirements [Dewes & Dimitrova, AAAI 2025]. SysML v2 [OMG, 2025] gives requirements an assume constraint and a require constraint, and SAWS² connects a fragment of SysML v2 to OCRA, nuXmv, and xSAP [FBK, SAWS²].

What remains open for CADL is the institutional interpretation of these operators. The parties of a CADL contract are autonomous organisations whose compliance depends on incentives rather than on the designer's authority; composition must therefore be indexed by institutional parameters and its guarantees must hold in equilibrium. Switching between whole operating modes (regimes), including the transitional phase, must be safe, and the algebra must stay open to constituents joining and leaving. For example, when composing household chore-sharing institution with shared resource usage institution, a theory that algebraically guarantees absence of obligation conflicts and incentive interference is required. Mode-aware contracts address components, and the evolution of SoS configurations has been checked without a contract algebra [Etzien+, ISORC 2013]. We found no work that models normative concepts (obligation, permission, sanction) in SysML v2.

The structure of the institutional parameter space (α, β, λ) is a second open area. Parameter synthesis for parametric Markov chains and MDPs already partitions a parameter space into regions certified to satisfy or to violate a property [Junges+, FMSD 2024]; Storm implements it and can be called from Python [Hensel+, STTT 2022], and monotonicity checking proves, region by region, that a probability increases or decreases with a parameter [Spel+, ATVA 2019]. These tools answer whether one design satisfies a threshold on a region. They do not answer which design is best where, which is the question of a regime map. The stochastic model extension (Appendix H, stochastic 0.1) is a first step on RD-1.3: it lets a file state a parametric Markov model in which α, β, and λ appear, defines the regime map as a query whose result is a set of certified cells, and fixes an export to the PRISM language. The reference implementation computes certified cells by parameter lifting (H.14). Deriving the model from contracts, scaling beyond small models, and the game reading of Section 9.7 remain open.

9.2 Extraction and Institutionalization of Tacit Knowledge​

Establishment of technology to explicitly formalize institutional knowledge implicitly shared in household rules and community customs as CADL descriptions is required. IndoorWorld [Wu+, Findings of EMNLP 2025] realizes behavior simulation of LLM agents in indoor environments, but the inverse problem of extracting institutional rules from observed behavior patterns (institutional reverse engineering) remains unaddressed. For timed properties, procedures that mine signal temporal logic specifications from traces have been surveyed [Bartocci+, Inf. Comput. 2022].

LLM-based translation into formal specifications is accurate only for simple targets. A fine-tuned model reached more than 95% accuracy in translating natural language into temporal logic with abstracted propositions, while GPT-4 with few-shot prompting reached 77.7% [Chen+, EMNLP 2023]. For TLA+, the best of thirty LLMs produced semantically correct specifications in 8.6% of the cases [Bisharat+, ICSOFT 2026]. The LLM-based generation of Symboleo [Zitouni+, 2025] assessed 38 prompt configurations against 16 error types and found the results promising but with many remaining errors. cadl ai already loops over parsing, type checking, and retries, and with --verify runs the SMT and deadlock checks; this assures syntactic validity and consistency, not that the description is faithful to the source text. We found no benchmark for translating deontic content (obligation, permission, remedy).

9.3 Institutional Digital Twins and Assurance​

Developing the concept of Digital Twins of Organization [Lyytinen+, J. Organization Design, 2024], establishment of technology to build and simulate institutions themselves as digital twins is the next-generation research direction. Lyytinen et al. argue that a full twin of an organization may not be feasible because organizations involve agency, conflict, and emergence; a reply argues that a twin built for a narrow purpose from digital trace data is feasible [Winkler & Kremser, J. Organization Design, 2024]. A CADL description can serve as the normative layer of such a narrow twin: it mirrors the agreed rules, not the whole organization. vCity [BSC] and Social Digital Twin [Fujitsu] advance twinning of physical and social aspects, but twinning of institutions (governance structures, rules, incentives) is, to our knowledge, not their main target.

Dynamic assurance cases connect a model to runtime evidence. Starting from dynamic safety cases [Denney+, ICSE 2015], ACCESS evaluates an assurance case both at design time and at runtime, where the validity of the case depends on runtime data checked by a monitor [Wei+, JSS 2024], and Trusta checks LLM-drafted assurance cases against formal constraints [Chen+, SCP 2025]. The notations are GSN Community Standard Version 3 [SCSC-141C, 2021] and OMG SACM 2.3 [OMG SACM, 2023]. The divergence between a model and the running system is studied as models@run.time [Bencomo+, SoSyM 2019].

Three problems remain open for CADL. First, the dynamic assurance cases we found assure a single technical system; we found none whose claims are the obligations, permissions, and amendment procedures of several organizations and whose evidence comes from monitors owned by different parties. Second, research on twin synchronization assumes that reality corrects the model. For an institution the model is normative: a divergence is either a violation by a participant (evidence against an assurance claim) or a drift in which the rules actually in force changed without an amendment of the CADL description (the twin is stale), and observation alone does not tell them apart. Updating the model automatically to fit observed practice would ratify violations. Third, statistical evaluation of institutions on a simulator needs confidence bounds, and experiments on institutions change the behaviour of the people who take part in them.

RD-3.1Institutional SimulatorTechnology to automatically generate institutional simulation models from CADL descriptions and what-if analyze impacts of institutional parameter changes. MAPF simulator integration is initial target. Wrap the simulator as a black-box oracle for statistical model checking, attach a confidence interval to each parameter point, and mark boundaries where the intervals of competing regimes overlap as statistically indistinguishable; export to JANI to reach both exact tools (RD-1.3) and statistical model checkers such as modes [Budde+, TACAS 2018].
RD-3.2Divergence Classification and
Connection to Amendment		Detect divergence between institutional operation and the CADL description by checking monitor traces against it, and classify each divergence as a violation or as a drift. A violation is handled by the contract (violation handling and sanctions) and counts as evidence against the assurance case. A drift is not corrected by updating the description automatically: it goes through the amendment procedure (in Course D, a constitution gate and a versioned record of amendments) and from there into the change management process of IEC 62853 [IEC 62853:2018].
RD-3.3Verified Institutional
A/B Testing		Technology to operate different institutional designs in parallel and compare their performance. Institutional parameters proposed by reinforcement learning, as in AI Economist [Zheng+, 2022], or by LLM agents are certified with the methods of RD-1.3 and RD-7.1 before they are deployed. The "rationality" of LLM agents is not the rationality assumed by game theory, and this must be stated as a precondition of any claim. Because experiments on institutions affect their participants, they need a framework of ethics and consent.
RD-3.4Assurance Cases
Generated from CADL		Derive the claims of an assurance case deterministically from contract elements, use design-time static checks and operation-time monitor verdicts as evidence, output the case in GSN [SCSC-141C, 2021] or SACM [OMG SACM, 2023], and use it as the dependability case of IEC 62853. Re-evaluate the case when monitors report, as ACCESS does for technical systems [Wei+, JSS 2024]. Open points: argument patterns for institutional claims (fairness, accountability, amendability), aggregating confidence over monitors owned by different organizations, and referring to the constituents' own assurance cases as away goals.

9.4 Scalability and Computational Complexity​

Scalability of formal verification and code generation of institutions is one of the most critical challenges toward practical deployment. To complete verification of SoS descriptions containing 100+ actors and 50+ contracts within practical time, theoretical analysis of computational complexity of verification methods and development of efficient algorithms are necessary. Some bounds are already known for the strategic questions of Section 9.7: checking LTL properties on the equilibria of probabilistic systems is 2EXPTIME-complete [Gutierrez+, KR 2021]; designing incentives so that a property holds on equilibria is in PSPACE for LTL and in NP or Σ₂ᴾ for GR(1) goals [Gutierrez+, LMCS 2024]; and verifying incentives with quantitative goals is 2EXPTIME-complete [Hyland+, KR 2024]. Practical tools will therefore need restricted fragments such as GR(1) [Piterman+, VMCAI 2006], finite traces, or bounded horizons.

IDChallengeContent
RD-4.1Computational Complexity of
Institutional Verification		Determine computational complexity classes for consistency verification, deadlock detection, safety verification, realizability of temporal contract sets, and equilibrium properties of CADL descriptions respectively, starting from the known results above. Identify decidability boundary conditions and the fragments (for example GR(1) [Piterman+, VMCAI 2006]) that keep them tractable.
RD-4.2Abstraction-based
Scalable Verification		Methods to efficiently verify large-scale SoS through hierarchical abstraction of institutional descriptions. Application of counterexample-guided abstraction refinement (CEGAR) [Clarke+, 2000] to institutional verification, and compositional assume-guarantee reasoning per constituent for the equilibrium checks of Section 9.7.
RD-4.3Incremental
Verification		Confirm consistency through differential verification rather than full re-verification when actors join or leave or institutions change. Formulate leaving as the quotient C_sys / C_rest (the specification that a replacement must satisfy given the remaining constituents) and joining as re-checking the refinement of the new composition, computable with Pacti [Incer+, arXiv 2023] for the fragments it supports. We found no work that formulates join and leave this way; earlier work on evolving SoS checks the reachability of critical configurations without a contract algebra [Etzien+, ISORC 2013].

9.5 Multi-Cultural and Multi-Jurisdictional Support​

For CADL to be applied to international SoS, support for different legal systems, cultural norms, and language environments is necessary. The EU Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) sets essential requirements for smart contracts used for the automated execution of data sharing agreements, but institutional compatibility with other jurisdictions (Japan, US, China, etc.) remains an unresolved challenge. For data usage, ODRL is used as the policy layer of data spaces [Iannella+, W3C Blog 2025], and the draft ODRL Formal Semantics of the W3C Community Group defines deontic states for permissions, prohibitions, and duties and separates access control from monitoring; it leaves remedies unformalized and the handling of conflicts pending [W3C ODRL CG, Formal Semantics]. We found no reliable source that maps the obligations of the EU Data Act to ODRL policies, and CADL does not assume such a mapping.

RD-5.1Cross-jurisdictional Institutional
Compatibility Verification		Technology to detect legal contradictions when composing CADL descriptions from different jurisdictions (e.g., differences between EU GDPR [Regulation (EU) 2016/679] and Japanese Personal Information Protection Act [Act No. 57 of 2003]). For data-sharing norms, refer to the conflict semantics of ODRL as it is formalized [W3C ODRL CG, Formal Semantics].
RD-5.2Parameterization of
Cultural Norms		Technology to model cultural dimensions (collectivism/individualism, power distance, uncertainty avoidance, etc.) as institutional parameters and handle cross-cultural governance differences quantitatively.
RD-5.3Multilingual Institutional DescriptionEnable CADL overview-level descriptions in multiple languages with AI verifying institutional equivalence across languages. This goes beyond NFR-7 (internationalization), which only requires that human-readable text can be written in any language.

9.6 Norms and Time​

CADL contracts describe normative agreements, but norms are not yet first-class objects with their own state over time. Symboleo [Sharifi+, 2020] treats obligations and powers as objects with lifecycles (creation, activation, suspension, resumption, fulfilment, violation, termination), SymboleoPC translates Symboleo to nuXmv and checks LTL and CTL properties [Parvizimosaed+, SoSyM 2025], and Symboleo specifications have been translated into smart contracts [Rasti+, MODELS 2022]. The normative programming language NPL handles obligations with deadlines and sanctions in multi-agent organisations [Hübner+, AMAI 2011], and constitutive norms (counts-as) create institutional facts from other facts, as distinct from regulative norms [Boella & van der Torre, KR 2004]. Norms can also be synthesized automatically for multi-agent systems [Alechina+, KR 2022].

On the side of time and monitoring, RTAMT computes the robustness of signal temporal logic online, and its interface-aware variant separates input signals from output signals, which matches CADL's split between assume and guarantee [Ničković & Yamaguchi, ATVA 2020] [Yamaguchi+, STTT 2024]. R2U2 compiles temporal specifications into hardware or software observers [Johannsen+, CAV 2023]. An assumption can make more properties monitorable by restricting the traces a monitor must handle [Henzinger & Saraç, RV 2020]. Deontic runtime verification identifies the party to blame [Kharraz+, ICTAC 2023] and distinguishes where a violation is detected from where it occurred [Colombo+, ISoLA 2024]. Monitoring has been decentralized [Francalanza+, 2018], and shields synthesized from temporal safety specifications block unsafe actions [Alshiekh+, AAAI 2018], also per agent without communication [Melcer+, NeurIPS 2022].

In the core language, assume and guarantee are state predicates without temporal operators, deadlines exist only on lifecycle transitions (deadline:), monitors are hand-written rule: predicates, and Z3 is used only for satisfiability. The norms extension (Appendix F, norms 0.1) is a first step on RD-6.1, RD-6.2, RD-6.3, and RD-6.5: it defines first-class norms, bounded temporal operators with four-valued verdicts, counts-as rules, and what happens to running norms on an amendment. The generated Python code monitors norms at run time (F.10). Design-time checking by a model checker, monitoring in the other generated runtimes, and the formal link to α, β, and λ remain open. We found no work that handles first-class norm objects together with the institutional parameters α, β, and λ, the composition of many contracts in an SoS, and amendment during operation. We also found no tool that connects deontic blame semantics with efficient monitoring engines, or that lets several organizations, each observing only its own interface with unsynchronized clocks, agree on a verdict and on the responsible party.

9.7 Strategic Constituents​

CADL assumes that constituent systems keep their operational and managerial independence, so they may not comply with a contract when compliance does not pay. Rational verification checks whether a temporal property holds on the Nash equilibria of a multi-agent system: EVE takes modules with LTL goals and reduces the problem to parity games [Gutierrez+, AI 2020], and the problem has been extended to probabilistic systems [Gutierrez+, KR 2021]. Recent work moved from checking to design: equilibrium design chooses incentives within a budget so that a property holds on equilibria [Gutierrez+, LMCS 2024], incentive design handles static and dynamic incentives with quantitative goals [Hyland+, KR 2024], and norms can be synthesized against strategic objectives [Alechina+, KR 2022]. PRISM-games verifies Nash and correlated equilibria of concurrent stochastic games with social-welfare and fairness criteria [Kwiatkowska+, TACAS 2022] and has been extended to psychological games, whose payoffs depend on beliefs [Kwiatkowska+, 2024]. Bayesian incentive compatibility of mechanisms has been reduced to model checking [Mittelmann+, AAAI 2023]. Learning-based institution design such as AI Economist [Zheng+, 2022] gives no formal guarantee.

What remains open for CADL is a map rather than a point. Equilibrium design finds one incentive scheme; CADL needs the range of λ (and of the motivation parameters ρ and κ, Appendix C) in which compliance is an equilibrium. We found no tool that combines parametric transition probabilities with equilibrium computation, and little work on equilibria when constituents observe only part of the state, which is what α controls.

Use restricted fragments (Section 9.4) to keep the checks tractable.

9.8 Governance of AI Agents​

LLM-based agents are becoming constituent systems of SoS, and the protocols that connect them are being standardized. The current specification of the Model Context Protocol (MCP) has a stateless core, requires Mcp-Method and Mcp-Name headers so that gateways can route and meter requests without parsing their bodies, and adds a framework for extensions [MCP, 2026-07-28]. MCP is a project of the Agentic AI Foundation of the Linux Foundation [Linux Foundation, AAIF 2025], and the Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol was contributed to the Linux Foundation [Linux Foundation, A2A 2025]. A preprint that compares MCP, A2A, and three other protocols finds that none of them supports voting or preserving dissent, and concludes that community governance is a layer missing above them [Kang & Diponegoro, arXiv 2026]. Enforcement is converging on deterministic checks at the tool-call boundary; for example, Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Policy authorizes tool calls with Cedar policies and denies by default [AWS, AgentCore Policy]. A preprint review of 38 studies finds that writing the specification is the bottleneck, that runtime monitoring is the most mature approach but gives guarantees for single steps, and reports a study in which blocking 94% of unsafe actions still left fewer than 5% of tasks completed safely and correctly [Dantas+, arXiv 2026]. From the side of institutional design, a preprint enforced a public governance graph with an append-only audit log and reduced severe collusion among LLM agents from 50% to 5.6% of runs, while a prompt-only "constitution" showed no reliable improvement [Bracale Syrnikov+, arXiv 2026]. Regulation such as the EU AI Act, with its requirements on record-keeping and human oversight, is a further motivation for executable governance; this chapter does not claim a mapping from its articles to CADL.

What remains open for CADL is the step from organizational contracts (roles, obligations, the provenance of authority, incentives) to deterministic enforcement at the protocol level; we found no work that takes this step. The institutional designs above are built by hand for each case, and cadl ai assures only the syntactic validity and consistency of what it generates (Section 9.2). The agent governance extension (Appendix G, agents 0.1) is a first step on RD-8.1, RD-8.2, and RD-8.3: it defines the decision that the norms of a contract give for a message at the boundary, delegation with a provenance chain, and escalation after a denial. Governance elements such as voting and dissent (RD-8.4) and plan-level verification (RD-8.5) remain open.
